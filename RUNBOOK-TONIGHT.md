# Runbook: Tonight

```text
Runbook:    D-VISTA-RB-TONIGHT
Duration:   7-10 minutes, then sleep
Requires:   Linksys WRT54GS (or any equivalent anchor)
            somewhere to sleep
            paper and pen, or a voice memo app
Does not
require:    power, a network, an alarm, a screen, any software
```

## Deployment

If you only have 90 seconds, run [BOOTSTRAP.md](../BOOTSTRAP.md) instead. This
runbook is the full version of the same installation. Either way, reading and
rehearsing happen while you're awake: they are preparation, not the experiment.
The experiment happens, or doesn't, while you sleep. Lucidity is not guaranteed.

## About this runbook

This is the whole protocol, written for a tired person. Follow it top to bottom.
If you are too tired for all of it, do the [90-second bootstrap](../BOOTSTRAP.md)
instead. Either counts.

## Before you start

- This is an experiment. Most nights nothing happens. That is fine.
- Sleep comes first. If any step keeps you awake, skip it and sleep.
- Do not touch medical alarms. Glucose monitors, insulin pump alerts and similar
  stay on, at their usual volume, where you can hear them.

## Step 1. Prepare (1 minute)

- Put the WRT54GS somewhere safe and dry near where you'll do the rehearsal: a
  desk, a shelf, the nightstand. **Not in the bed.**
- It does **not** need to be plugged in. Do not reconfigure it, flash it, or
  touch your real network.
- Put paper and a pen (or your phone, screen dimmed, voice memo ready) within
  reach of the bed.
- Silence nonessential notifications if you normally do. **Leave medical alerts
  alone.**
- Dim the lights. No bright screens from here on.

## Step 2. Handle the anchor (1 minute)

Pick up the router. Spend about a minute with it.

- Feel the weight and the plastic.
- Look at the two antennas and their angle. Turn one.
- Look at the blue and black casing and the row of indicator lights on the
  front.
- Say, quietly or in your head: **"This means I have Wi-Fi."**

You are not memorizing the router. You are attaching a feeling to a fact:
wireless is available.

## Step 3. Train the response (2 minutes)

Run this sequence slowly, about five times:

```text
notice Wi-Fi  ->  ask "Am I dreaming?"  ->  examine the situation  ->  recall bedtime context
```

Each time:

1. Notice: "I have Wi-Fi."
2. Ask: "Am I dreaming?"
3. Examine: Look around the room. How did I get here? Does everything make
   sense? Is it stable if I look away and back?
4. Recall: "I'm doing this before bed on purpose."
5. Answer honestly: **"No, I'm awake."**

Do it with attention, not on autopilot. The goal is that noticing Wi-Fi makes
you actually look, not just recite words.

## Step 4. Practice three imagined scenarios (2-3 minutes)

Put the router down. Close your eyes. For each scenario, imagine it for about
30 seconds, and this time end with recognizing the dream.

**A. Your bedroom.** You're in your room, but something is off: the window is
in the wrong place. You realize you have Wi-Fi. You ask, "Am I dreaming?" You
look: the window is wrong, and you can't remember getting here. **"This is a
dream."**

**B. An unfamiliar place.** A big building you've never been in. Your phone is
connected. You ask the question. You don't know how you got here. **"This is a
dream."**

**C. No router, no screen.** You're somewhere with nothing technological at all:
a field, a beach, an empty street. Nobody mentions it, nothing shows it, but you
simply *know* there's Wi-Fi here. That knowing is the cue. You ask. You look
around. **"This is a dream."**

Scenario C is the important one. The dream does not need to show you anything.

## Step 5. Lie down and set the intention (until you fall asleep)

Lie down comfortably. Say once or twice, slowly:

> **"When I notice I have Wi-Fi, I'll remember to check whether I'm dreaming."**

Then let it go. Let sleep come normally. **Do not try to stay awake**, and do
not keep repeating the phrase if it's keeping you up. If you're still awake
after a while, stop rehearsing entirely and just rest.

## Step 6. If you become lucid

1. Stay calm. Think: "This is a dream."
2. Look around for a few moments. Notice detail.
3. Do one thing. The plan for tonight: find any keyboard and type `ssh fridge`,
   or say it aloud. **This is a dreamed action.** It connects to nothing.
   Whatever appears is dream content.
4. If you can't find a keyboard, that's fine. Just observe.
5. If the dream turns unpleasant, look away, remind yourself it's a dream, or
   gently wake up.

## Step 7. When you wake up

Before you check your phone or get up:

1. Lie still a moment and replay what you remember.
2. Write it down or record it. Keywords are enough.
3. Answer two separate questions:
   - Did Wi-Fi come up in the dream, in any form?
   - Did I **know** I was dreaming, while dreaming?
4. Score the night:

| Score | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `0` | Nothing recalled |
| `1` | Dream recalled |
| `2` | Wi-Fi concept appeared |
| `3` | Wi-Fi led to recognition of dreaming |
| `4` | Lucid, and the intended action was attempted |

5. If it's still night, go back to sleep. If you woke naturally and feel like
   it, you can use [natural-awakening.md](RUNBOOK-NATURAL-AWAKENING.md). Don't force it.

Use the template in
[DREAM-JOURNAL-TEMPLATE.md](DREAM-JOURNAL-TEMPLATE.md) if you
want structure.

## Step 8. If this is making things worse

If the routine makes you anxious, keeps you awake, wakes you up, or brings
nightmares: **stop, and just sleep.** Nothing is lost. The details are in
[RFC 0006](RFC-0006-SECURITY-CONSIDERATIONS.md).

## Checklist

```text
[ ] Router somewhere safe, not in bed, not reconfigured
[ ] Paper/pen or voice memo within reach
[ ] Medical alerts untouched
[ ] Lights dim, screens away
[ ] Handled the router: "This means I have Wi-Fi."
[ ] Response x5: notice -> ask -> examine -> recall -> "No, I'm awake."
[ ] Scenarios: bedroom, unfamiliar place, no router at all
[ ] Lie down: "When I notice I have Wi-Fi, I'll remember to check whether I'm dreaming."
[ ] Sleep
[ ] On waking: write first, score 0-4, back to sleep
```
