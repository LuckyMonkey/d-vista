# Implementation Notes

This page describes the cognitive mechanism directly. It says plainly what D-VISTA is, what it is not,
and what is actually happening when you use it.

## What D-VISTA is

A lucid-dreaming practice written in the style of software documentation. The
practice is real and combines established ideas:

- **Cue rehearsal.** You pick one thing, noticing that Wi-Fi is available, and
  rehearse responding to it by asking "Am I dreaming?"
- **Prospective memory.** This is the ability to remember to do something when
  a particular moment arrives. MILD (mnemonic induction of lucid dreams) uses it
  to carry an intention into sleep.
- **Dream incubation.** Thinking about a theme before sleep can make it more
  likely to show up in dreams, though never reliably.
- **A state check.** When the cue appears, you look at your situation honestly.
  In a dream, that check can lead to realizing you are dreaming. That
  realization is lucidity.

The novel part, using the *idea* of having Wi-Fi as the cue with a WRT54GS as a
physical reminder, is an untested personal experiment. There is no research on
it.

## What D-VISTA is not

- **Not software.** No executable program is installed. "Installation" means you
  read an instruction and decided to remember it. You can uninstall it by not
  doing the rehearsal.
- **Not a network.** No Wi-Fi signal, packet or GitHub traffic is involved
  during sleep. The router can be unplugged. A dream about downloading this
  repository is a dream.
- **Not DNS.** Real DNS is a deterministic system. Its answers can depend on
  which resolver you ask (split-horizon DNS), but they are not a matter of
  perspective. dDNS borrows the vocabulary as a metaphor for how dreams assign
  meanings.
- **Not guaranteed.** Neither the router nor the text guarantees a lucid dream.
  Most nights will not produce one. A dream that contains Wi-Fi is not a lucid
  dream unless you recognize it as a dream.
- **Not hidden.** Nothing here works on you without your participation, and
  nothing is meant to.

## Why protocol terminology helps

The software framing is a mnemonic device too. A rule that arrives as an
"install procedure" with a status screen is more memorable than the same rule in
a self-help paragraph, and memorability is the whole job of prospective memory.
The technical metaphors reinforce a reproducible cue-and-response sequence.

## Sleep comes first

- Keep your normal bedtime and sleep length.
- Keep bright screens out of the last part of the evening where you can.
- No alarms to interrupt sleep; no staying up; no alcohol, cannabis,
  supplements or medication changes for this.
- Never silence or move medical alarms, such as glucose monitor or insulin pump
  alerts.
- If you lie awake rehearsing, stop rehearsing.

Stop entirely if sleep gets worse, bedtime becomes stressful, nightmares or
frightening sleep paralysis increase, or you find it harder to tell dreams from
memories. If problems continue after stopping, talk to a doctor.

## Logging

Dream memory fades within minutes of waking and is easily reshaped. Write down
what you remember straight away, briefly, before checking your phone. Record two
facts separately: whether Wi-Fi appeared, and whether you knew you were
dreaming. Do not upgrade a night's result later because it feels more lucid in
hindsight. The template is
[DREAM-JOURNAL-TEMPLATE.md](DREAM-JOURNAL-TEMPLATE.md).
