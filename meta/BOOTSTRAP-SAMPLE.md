# D-VISTA / BOOTSTRAP

**Installation medium:** this document  
**Target architecture:** human prospective memory  
**Installation privilege:** voluntary attention  
**Service availability:** scheduled downtime only

D-VISTA ships without an executable. Its only installation procedure is to rehearse an intention that may be recalled in a dream.

### Phase 0 — Install (while awake)

If a Linksys WRT54GS is available, look at it or hold it briefly. It is a physical reminder of wireless connectivity, not a transmitter into the dream. No powered radio is required.

Become aware of the fact that Wi-Fi is available somewhere around you. Then ask, seriously, **“Am I dreaming?”** Check where you are and how you arrived here. While awake, answer honestly: **“No. I am awake.”**

Memorize the rule:

> **When I notice that I have Wi-Fi, I remember to check whether I'm dreaming.**

### Phase 1 — Prepare the runtime

Close your eyes and briefly imagine three different places: your bedroom, an unfamiliar building, and a featureless dream landscape. In each, no router or phone is needed. You simply *know* Wi-Fi is available. Rehearse remembering the rule and recognizing, in the imagined scenario, that you are dreaming.

Don't force images or prolong the rehearsal. Once the rule feels familiar, go to sleep normally. No alarm or sleep disruption is required.

### Phase 2 — Deferred activation (not guaranteed)

If a dream makes you aware of Wi-Fi, ask whether you are dreaming. Knowing that a dream contains Wi-Fi is not by itself lucidity. Lucidity occurs only if you realize **this experience is a dream**.

If that happens, observe your surroundings and attempt one simple intended action. Upon waking, record what you remember.

The dreaming brain may imagine a network interface, a terminal, or even pulling this repository from GitHub. These would be remembered or reconstructed dream experiences, **not real network activity**.
