# Evidence Register — D-VISTA

Status: literature reviewed 2026-10-20. This document tracks *claims*, not just citations. A study demonstrating dream-content manipulation does **not** demonstrate lucid-dream induction. An N1 insight study does **not** establish that an object-drop technique creates lucid dreams.

## Evidence classification

- **Supported:** replicated or substantively supported in published studies; not guaranteed for an individual.
- **Promising:** published positive findings with constraints, mixed methods, or limited replication.
- **Demonstrated for another outcome:** evidence supports the named outcome, not D-VISTA's primary outcome.
- **Hypothesis:** project-specific suggestion without direct experimental validation.
- **Historical:** documented practice or anecdote, not an efficacy study.

| Claim or method | Status | Evidence and limits | D-VISTA decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **MILD**, rehearsing a future intention to notice dreaming | **Supported for increasing chances of lucidity**, not reliable induction | 2023 review identifies MILD as best-supported cognitive approach; 2020 ILDIS (N=355) reports MILD and SSILD similarly effective in its design; results vary across participants | **Primary induction mechanism:** pair the Wi-Fi cue with questioning the dream state |
| **WBTB + MILD** | **Promising in combination** | Much of the supporting literature combines the two; effects cannot always be separated. Interrupted sleep may be undesirable | Optional *natural awakening* only in v0.1; never require a sleep-interruption alarm |
| **Reality testing alone** | **Insufficient/ambiguous** | Reviews do not establish consistent efficacy alone | Only an occasional, grounded response to Wi-Fi awareness; never treat Wi-Fi as proof of dreaming |
| **SSILD** | **Promising but less replicated** | Positive results in ILDIS; limited independent study and generally paired with WBTB | Referenced as an alternative; not required |
| **Dream journaling** | **Useful for measurement; uncertain as induction alone** | Provides recall reports, which are essential to assess the experiment; alone it is not a reliable induction method | Record cue presence and lucidity **separately** |
| **N1 sleep and creative insight** | **Demonstrated for a specific insight task, not lucidity** | Lacaux et al. (2021), N=103: after ≥15 seconds N1, 83% subsequently found a hidden mathematical shortcut vs 30% in a wake control; advantage did not persist into deeper sleep. Task-specific result, not general IQ improvement | Optional **N1 Capture** runbook for retrieving imagery/ideas, distinct from LCP |
| **Dalí's key / Edison's ball-drop** | **Historical technique** | They are associated with catching the transition into sleep by dropping a held object; the physical trick itself has no established success rate for lucid dreaming | Historical inspiration only. Do not require a metal key, steel ball, or disruptive awakenings |
| **Targeted Dream Incubation (TDI / Dormio)** | **Demonstrated for influencing sleep-onset dream themes** | Horowitz et al. (2020) used a wearable and sound prompts; 2023 work found theme-related creative benefits following incubation | Supports *dream-content* pathway; does not directly validate the Wi-Fi cue |
| **Targeted Lucidity Reactivation (TLR)** | **Promising for lucid-dream induction with trained external cues** | Konkoly et al. (2024) trained a lucidity-oriented response to sounds and replayed cues during sleep; reported increased lucidity vs appropriate controls | **Future research track**, not a DIY overnight audio implementation |
| **Conceptual Wi-Fi discovery + WRT54GS anchor** | **D-VISTA hypothesis, untested** | No identified controlled experiment validates Wi-Fi as a dream cue or a specific router as a mnemonic aid | Core n=1 experiment; present as hypothesis, not established science |
| **“Dream pulls repository from GitHub”** | **Narrative metaphor, not a physical claim** | Dreams can reconstruct prior experiences; no network or software executes in sleeping cognition as described | Keep the fiction legible; explicitly distinguish subjective dream content from external downloads |

## Do not confuse endpoints

The following outcomes are *not interchangeable*:

1. **N1 capture:** The operator recalls an image, idea, or association immediately after drifting off.
2. **Dream incorporation:** Wi-Fi, the router, or the documentation appeared as dream content.
3. **Lucid dreaming:** While dreaming, the operator explicitly recognized that they were dreaming.
4. **Dream task completion:** After recognizing lucidity, the operator attempted a planned task.
5. **External fact:** Any claim about an actual server, program, network, or message must be independently checked while awake.

A successful N1 capture **does not** satisfy the primary LCP success criterion. Record it independently.

## Experiment design recommendations

- Settle the question before sleep: **Did the Wi-Fi cue occur? Did it lead to recognition?**
- Keep a **baseline** (dream recall without the routine) and compare with the protocol nights if practical.
- Log **sleep quality and distress** alongside successes. A method that worsens sleep is a failed intervention for this project.
- Record N1 attempts on a separate line from overnight dream sessions. They sample different states and endpoints.
- Avoid success inflation: ambiguous recall stays ambiguous; dream imagery is not an externally verified observation.
- No drugs or supplements are recommended here, even where research literature has studied them.
- Do not claim clinical effectiveness, diagnostic capacity, guaranteed lucidity, or cross-state network communication.
