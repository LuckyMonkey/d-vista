# Replication and Independent Verification

Revision: 2026-10-20. D-VISTA is an exploratory protocol. Its present data collection is self-report based; this document provides increasingly strong validation paths without presuming success.

## Level 1 — Individual feasibility

Use [STUDY.md](STUDY.md) and [OBSERVATIONS.csv](OBSERVATIONS.csv). Confirm that the operator can remember the Wi-Fi rule while awake, complete the bedtime protocol without sacrificing sleep, and log consecutive mornings. This validates **practical adherence**, not dream induction.

## Level 2 — Repeated, predeclared self-observation

Use baseline → training → follow-up, with dates fixed before viewing outcomes. Include zero recall and interruptions in reporting. Score [E0–E4](MEASUREMENT.md) with stable definitions and preserve contemporaneous accounts. Compare cue frequency, lucidity, and adverse effects as separate measures. Successful cue incorporation is not proof of a triggered lucid episode.

## Level 3 — Multiple independent operators

Publish the same protocol, but collect consenting reports without names, clinical details, locations, or identifiable dream narratives. Allow participants to opt out and delete unshared source records. Before analysis, record eligibility and exclusions, baseline lucid-dream frequency, and missingness. Avoid pooling nights as if they came from independent people.

For a future controlled pilot, consider two groups with equivalent sleep and journaling instructions, one using Wi-Fi awareness and one using a preselected **comparison concept** (for example, “I can hear music”). Match rehearsal burden; predeclare the comparison. The aim is to isolate cue-specific effects from expectancy and memory rehearsal.

## Level 4 — Objective laboratory measurement

Studies show that lucid dreaming can be investigated while participants are asleep, using polysomnography (PSG) and pre-agreed eye or facial-muscle signals. **This does not establish that D-VISTA already works.**

A qualified sleep laboratory could test the following:

1. Establish an agreed left-right-left-right eye signal and demonstrate it while awake.
2. Monitor EEG, EOG and EMG using laboratory standards for sleep staging.
3. Use the ordinary D-VISTA waking training protocol without inducing sleep deprivation.
4. Identify a REM interval with a distinctive volitional signal that is unlikely to be an ordinary eye movement.
5. Obtain a prompt post-awakening report of **how lucidity began** and whether the Wi-Fi cue preceded recognition.
6. Align the time-stamped objective signal and subjective report; report agreement and ambiguities.

The signal would support **conscious behavior during recorded sleep**. The claim that Wi-Fi triggered recognition still relies on subjective sequencing unless new independently observable methods are developed.

Reference: Konkoly et al. (2021), [Real-time dialogue between experimenters and dreamers during REM sleep](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2021.01.026); different groups independently recorded time-coded correct replies using eye movements and muscle signals.

## Sensor and radio considerations

- **Consumer wearables** may help estimate sleep schedules; most cannot definitively confirm an N1/REM epoch or lucidity.
- **Wi-Fi hardware and radio power** are not causal mechanisms in v0.1/v0.2. No overnight packet collection is needed.
- **Active acoustic or visual stimulation** belongs in a later ethically evaluated study. The at-home TLR literature uses trained sound cues, not Wi-Fi transmissions.
- **N1 object-drop capture** is an optional, separately recorded creative-recall exercise; dropping an object is not sleep-stage identification.

## Null outcomes and falsifiability

Predictions can be contradicted by data: Wi-Fi cues might fail to enter dreams; cues may appear without lucidity; lucidity may arise without cues; or sleep could worsen. A lack of an observed effect is a limit on the tested protocol, not proof that no lucid-dream practice could ever work. Similarly, being unable to observe a particular dream event does not establish that the event occurred.

## Publication standard

Report protocol version, dates, recruitment, total scheduled nights, observed nights, recall rates, denominators, adverse effects, raw anonymized coding where consent permits, and deviations from the declared design. Separate hypotheses from observed results. Label illustrative accounts prominently.
